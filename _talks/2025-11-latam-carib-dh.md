## Abstract

This project combines Computational Linguistics (CL) / Digital Humanities (DH) methodologies to develop a user-friendly digital platform serving as a hub for the revitalization, promotion, and dissemination of Martinican Creole (MC) and Haitian Creole (HC) languages. The website will provide access to curated corpora and offer intuitive tools for students and scholars in the humanities and social sciences, to explore linguistic, cultural, and historical content. At the backend, a comprehensive natural language processing (NLP) toolkit—including a parser, part-of-speech (POS) tagger, tokenizer, and lemmatizer—is being developed specifically for MC and HC. These NLP tools not only enable detailed linguistic analysis but also reintroduce Creole languages into contemporary NLP research, traditionally dominated by high-resource languages.
This presentation will focus specifically on the initial stages of the project, highlighting the creation of foundational linguistic tools and preliminary design of the digital platform. It emphasizes the project's role in bridging language, culture, and technology, promoting interdisciplinary collaboration, and strengthening community engagement through increased accessibility to linguistic knowledge. Ultimately, this initiative exemplifies how combining computational linguistics with digital humanities can actively address both technological and cultural gaps, enhancing visibility and scholarly engagement with the rich linguistic and cultural heritage of the Caribbean.
