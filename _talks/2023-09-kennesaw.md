## Abstract

Personal assistants, and very recently ChatGPT (OpenAI), have shaken the entire world with their ability to understand human language and produce human-like utterances, and it has become evident that artificial intelligence (AI) plays and will continue to play an important role in our everyday life. However, because AIs do not “think” like humans, common knowledge and reasoning can sometimes get lost in translation and we are still far from systems capable of understanding the world the way humans naturally do.
Artificial intelligence and personal assistants (Siri, Alexa, Google assistant) are plural systems that lie at the intersection of linguistics and technology, and Computational linguistics (CL) is the field that combines these facets of AI. In this talk, I will describe the inherent hybrid nature of AI systems, show how my research in CL contributes to bridging the gap between AIs and humans, and how I use the latest technologies to create and develop language resources and computational tools for under-resourced languages, especially creole languages.
