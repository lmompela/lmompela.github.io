## Abstract
Developing natural language processing (NLP) tools for Creole languages faces a central challenge: limited annotated resources and high structural divergence from dominant languages. This presentation reports on a series of experiments designed to address these challenges by adapting recent computational approaches to the specific typologies and sociolinguistic realities of Creole languages. I present the first benchmark study of part-of-speech tagging for Martinican Creole, where detailed error analysis highlights systematic weaknesses of state-of-the-art language models, including difficulties with homonymy and polyfunctional morphemes. Building on this, I examine how Haitian Creole and Martinican Creole can support each other in syntactic parsing: by training models on both languages together, their shared structures make up for the lack of data in each language alone. In parallel, I present ongoing work on the development of an annotation schema for Haitian Creole medical corpora, designed to capture variation and evaluate translation quality in healthcare contexts. Across these studies, a consistent finding is that purely data-driven methods fail to adequately account for the diversity and complexity of Creole languages; integrating linguistic knowledge into model design and evaluation yields more interpretable and culturally grounded tools. I conclude by considering how these research directions support community-centered applications, such as healthcare translation and digital platforms for Creole NLP.
